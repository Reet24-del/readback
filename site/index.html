Findings

Five bugs, each with a reproduction and audio

BUG-001

Indian digit grouping is misread on every voice

₹10,00,000 → "ten zero rupees" · ₹1,50,000 → "one fifty thousand rupees"

Every amount of a lakh or more written with Indian commas (72 of 72 across three voices). The same amount written ₹150,000 is read correctly.

critical
BUG-002

OTPs, PNRs and pincodes are read as amounts

OTP 482910 → "four hundred eighty two thousand nine hundred ten" · PNR 4521367890 → "four billion…"

Every OTP, PNR and pincode, on all three voices. Hyphens in phone numbers become "minus". Leading zeros vanish, so 033705 becomes a different code.

critical
BUG-003

DD/MM dates are read in US order

03/04/2026 → "March fourth" · 01.02.1990 → "January second"

On a British English voice, where day-first is the standard too. An appointment on 3 April becomes one on 4 March.

high
BUG-004

Letter runs in codes may be pronounced as words

IFSC WYXK0992832 → "Wix zero nine…" · PAN IYPPV5695G → "Apu five six…"

Unconfirmed: the transcriber could be at fault here, so this one waits for a listening check.

needs ear check
BUG-005

Hidden characters make the voice and the screen disagree

screen ₹500 → voice "five" (right-to-left override) · screen ₹5 → voice "sub alias fifty thousand rupees" (SSML)

43 of 63 attack results change what is spoken without changing what is shown, including invisible characters that drop the last digits of an OTP.

security

A formatted transcript hides the bug

Checking TTS with formatted speech-to-text turns "one fifty thousand rupees" straight back into "₹1,50,000", so the test passes while the listener hears nonsense. Readback scores the word-for-word transcript (format_text=false) instead.

How it works

Speak it, hear it back, check every value

  1. Write the case

    The message, the critical part, and what it means: ₹10,00,000 is 1,000,000 rupees.

  2. Speak it

    AssemblyAI Voice Agent API. The text goes in as the greeting, which is spoken word for word with no LLM in between.

  3. Hear it back

    AssemblyAI Universal with format_text=false, so numbers come back as the words actually said.

  4. Score it

    Lakh-aware number parsing and checkers for amounts, doses, digits, codes, dates, times and emails. Anything unclear goes to a human listener.

The fix

SafeSpeak: say what the screen shows, or say nothing

  1. Match the screen

    Lays out right-to-left overrides as displayed, strips invisible characters and markup, and maps look-alike letters and digits.

  2. Spell values out

    "₹1,50,000" becomes "one lakh fifty thousand rupees", and an OTP becomes "four eight two, nine one zero".

  3. Verify the audio

    Transcribes what was actually spoken and checks every value against the text.

  4. Hold back

    If a value still can't be verified after a slower retry, it isn't spoken. The app sends an SMS instead.

What this does and doesn't show

Limits

  • Speech-to-text can be wrong too. Every failure is flagged for a listening check, and only BUG-001 has been confirmed by ear so far.
  • There is no Indian English voice yet, so the tests use British and American voices.
  • AssemblyAI's standalone TTS model (TTS-1) isn't released yet. These results are for the Voice Agent API's voices.
